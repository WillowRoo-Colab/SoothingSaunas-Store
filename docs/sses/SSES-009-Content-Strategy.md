# 1. Purpose
This specification establishes the approved content strategy for Soothing Saunas. It ensures that product copy, educational material, buying guidance, journal content, and search-oriented writing consistently support the company's guest-first mission while remaining accurate, useful, readable, and maintainable.
 
# 2. Scope
This standard applies to guest-facing written content created, commissioned, edited, imported, or published for Soothing Saunas, including:
- Homepage, landing-page, collection-page, and campaign copy.
- Product titles, summaries, descriptions, specifications, feature explanations, care guidance, and frequently asked questions.
- Educational articles, journal posts, health-supporting documentation, and general wellness guidance.
- Buying guides, comparison content, glossary entries, category introductions, and decision-support content.
- Email, customer-support, quiz, promotional, and interface microcopy when it forms part of the storefront experience.
- Search-oriented copy, keyword use, headings, excerpts, and internal content references.
 
This document governs content meaning, voice, evidence, structure, and editorial control. Technical URL architecture, metadata implementation, schema, taxonomy, and indexing rules are governed by SSES-010.
 
# 3. Governing Content Principles
| Principle | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Guest Utility** | Every material content element shall help a guest understand, compare, choose, use, or care for a product or make a better-informed wellness decision. |
| **Education Before Pressure** | Content shall explain before it persuades. Sales language shall not obscure limitations, prerequisites, costs, maintenance needs, or suitability considerations. |
| **Truthful Specificity** | Claims shall be precise, supportable, and proportionate to available evidence. Generalized superlatives and unsupported guarantees are prohibited. |
| **Brand Consistency** | Content shall reflect the Soothing Saunas philosophy, approved terminology, and guest experience standards without becoming repetitive or scripted. |
| **Progressive Disclosure** | Essential decisions shall be understandable quickly, while deeper technical, health, and comparison detail remains available for guests who need it. |
| **Maintainable Authority** | Published content shall have a clear source, owner, review condition, and revision path so outdated information does not quietly remain active. |
 
# 4. Audience, Voice, and Terminology
## 4.1 Primary Audience
Content shall be written for guests who may range from first-time wellness shoppers to experienced sauna owners. It shall not assume prior technical knowledge unless the page is explicitly identified as advanced reference material.
 
## 4.2 Approved Voice
| Voice Attribute | Application |
|---|---|
| **Calm** | Use measured language and avoid urgency tactics, fear-based framing, or exaggerated emotional pressure. |
| **Knowledgeable** | Explain mechanisms, differences, requirements, and tradeoffs clearly. Define specialized terms at first use. |
| **Supportive** | Anticipate common concerns and guide the guest without talking down to them. |
| **Transparent** | State what is known, what varies, what depends on product configuration, and what requires professional advice. |
| **Practical** | Prioritize usable guidance, decision criteria, dimensions, installation needs, care expectations, and ownership realities. |
 
## 4.3 Required Terminology
- Use `guest` rather than `customer` in editorial and experience-oriented content unless legal, transactional, or system terminology requires otherwise.
- Use product-category terminology consistently across products, guides, navigation, and support content.
- Define technical terms such as infrared wavelength, heater type, electrical requirement, capacity, and indoor/outdoor rating where ambiguity could affect a purchase.
- Avoid medicalized language that implies diagnosis, treatment, cure, prevention, or guaranteed physiological outcomes unless the statement is legally permissible and supported by an approved authoritative source.
 
# 5. Content Hierarchy and Page Responsibilities
| Content Layer | Primary Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Summary Layer** | Communicate the primary value, intended use, key differentiator, and most important purchase constraint in a concise form. |
| **Decision Layer** | Provide comparisons, specifications, suitability criteria, installation needs, included items, exclusions, and ownership considerations. |
| **Evidence Layer** | Provide supporting explanations, citations, references, limitations, and source context for health or technical claims. |
| **Action Layer** | Offer the next appropriate action, such as viewing specifications, comparing options, asking a question, adding to cart, or proceeding to checkout. |
| **Support Layer** | Provide care, warranty, returns, troubleshooting, contact, and post-purchase guidance where relevant. |
 
A page shall not force every layer into the opening screen. The hierarchy shall present essential decision information first and allow detail to expand naturally.
 
# 6. Product Content Standard
## 6.1 Required Product Information
- Accurate product name, category, manufacturer or brand where applicable, and model or variant identity.
- Plain-language summary of what the product is, who it is designed for, and the primary reason a guest would consider it.
- Key features translated into guest-relevant benefits without converting features into unsupported health promises.
- Dimensions, capacity, power, installation, location, compatibility, included components, and other decision-critical specifications.
- Known exclusions, required accessories, site preparation, assembly, professional installation, or electrical/plumbing requirements.
- Shipping, lead-time, warranty, return, or made-to-order considerations when they materially affect the purchase decision.
- Care, cleaning, operating, and safety information appropriate to the product.
 
## 6.2 Product Description Rules
- Lead with differentiation and suitability, not a generic category definition.
- Separate verified specifications from interpretive benefit statements.
- Do not copy supplier language without review for accuracy, readability, consistency, and unsupported claims.
- Do not hide material limitations in accordion content, fine print, images, or policy pages when the limitation can affect suitability or cost.
- Variants shall not inherit text that becomes inaccurate for a specific size, heater, material, voltage, color, or configuration.
 
# 7. Educational and Health-Supporting Content
Educational content shall help guests understand wellness concepts, product use, research context, and practical considerations. It shall support informed decision-making without presenting the storefront as a substitute for individualized medical care.
 
## 7.1 Evidence Classification
| Evidence Level | Permitted Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Established** | May be stated directly when the source and scope are reliable and the wording does not exceed the evidence. |
| **Supported but Limited** | May be discussed with qualifying language that explains study size, population, method, or uncertainty. |
| **Emerging** | May be presented as an area of interest or ongoing study, never as settled fact or guaranteed outcome. |
| **Traditional or Experiential** | May be described as historical, traditional, practitioner-reported, or user-reported experience when clearly distinguished from clinical evidence. |
| **Unsupported** | Shall not be presented as factual, therapeutic, or product-specific benefit. |
 
## 7.2 Health Claim Requirements
- Health statements shall identify whether they describe general wellness support, observed association, proposed mechanism, research finding, or established clinical use.
- The scope of the evidence shall not be expanded from a modality to a specific product without support.
- Risk, contraindication, hydration, heat exposure, medication, pregnancy, cardiovascular, pediatric, and other relevant cautions shall be included or linked where appropriate.
- Content shall advise consultation with an appropriate licensed professional when individual conditions, symptoms, medications, or treatment decisions materially affect safe use.
- Disclaimers shall supplement accurate content; they shall not be used to excuse misleading or exaggerated claims.
 
# 8. Buying Guides and Comparison Content
Buying guides shall reduce uncertainty by helping guests choose among product categories, technologies, sizes, configurations, and ownership models.
- State the decision being made and the guest factors that should influence it.
- Compare products using consistent criteria and equivalent units.
- Distinguish objective specifications from editorial judgment.
- Explain tradeoffs rather than declaring a universal winner when suitability depends on space, budget, climate, installation, use pattern, or preference.
- Disclose when a guide includes only products sold by Soothing Saunas or when commercial relationships may affect available options.
- Link to deeper educational material rather than repeating large blocks of identical text across multiple guides.
 
# 9. Journal and Blog Content
Journal content shall extend the educational mission of Soothing Saunas and create durable reference material rather than serving only as short-lived promotional copy.
 
| Content Type | Editorial Expectation |
|---|---|
| **Explainer** | Clarify a technology, wellness concept, product category, or ownership requirement. |
| **Research Summary** | Translate a study or body of evidence into accessible language while preserving limitations and source context. |
| **How-To** | Provide a safe, ordered process with prerequisites, materials, cautions, and expected outcome. |
| **Comparison** | Evaluate meaningful differences using declared criteria. |
| **Guest Question** | Answer a recurring question directly, then provide context and links to relevant products or guides. |
| **Seasonal or Campaign** | Remain useful and accurate beyond the campaign wherever practical; expiration-sensitive statements require review dates. |
 
# 10. Search-Oriented Writing
Content shall be discoverable without sacrificing clarity or trust. Search terms shall be integrated only when they reflect the actual subject and guest intent.
- Write headings and opening paragraphs for human comprehension first.
- Use natural variations of important terms rather than repetitive keyword insertion.
- Avoid creating near-duplicate pages solely to target minor keyword variations.
- Each page shall have a distinct purpose, primary question, and content contribution.
- Internal links shall use descriptive anchor text and connect guests to the next relevant explanation, comparison, product, or support resource.
- Technical metadata, canonicalization, structured data, indexation, and URL rules shall comply with SSES-010.
 
# 11. Readability and Content Structure
- Use descriptive headings that allow a guest to scan the page and understand its structure.
- Keep paragraphs focused on one idea and use tables or lists when comparison or sequence is clearer than prose.
- Place decision-critical facts near the relevant decision, not only in a separate specification table or policy page.
- Use consistent units and provide conversions when the guest audience reasonably requires them.
- Avoid unnecessary jargon, filler introductions, repeated brand slogans, and vague statements that do not help a decision.
- Microcopy shall state what an action does and what the guest should expect next.
 
# 12. Sources, Citations, and Attribution
- Material health, safety, performance, or technical claims shall be traceable to an approved source.
- Primary sources, official manufacturer documentation, recognized standards, and authoritative public guidance are preferred over unsourced summaries.
- Citations shall identify enough information to locate and evaluate the source.
- Quoted or licensed material shall comply with copyright, attribution, and usage permissions.
- Supplier data shall be treated as a source to verify, not automatic publication authority.
- When reliable sources disagree, content shall present the uncertainty or scope difference rather than silently selecting the most commercially favorable interpretation.
 
# 13. Content Workflow and Governance
| Stage | Required Control |
|---|---|
| **Brief** | Define audience, page purpose, decision supported, required facts, source needs, owner, and related SSES requirements. |
| **Draft** | Create content using approved voice, structure, terminology, and evidence treatment. |
| **Verification** | Confirm specifications, claims, citations, links, variant applicability, and policy alignment. |
| **Editorial Review** | Review clarity, usefulness, consistency, readability, and duplication. |
| **Approval** | Obtain owner approval or delegated approval before publication when the content establishes a new material claim, standard, or guest commitment. |
| **Publication** | Publish through the approved content system with version or revision traceability where supported. |
| **Maintenance** | Review when a source, product, policy, regulation, integration, or business decision changes. |
 
Material content changes shall not be made only in code, supplier feeds, or temporary prompts when they alter approved claims, promises, terminology, or decision guidance.
 
# 14. Content Ownership and System Boundaries
| Area | Authority |
|---|---|
| **Shopify Product Data** | Shopify remains the system of record for commerce data designated by SSES-006, including price, inventory, variants, and checkout-controlled information. |
| **Storefront Editorial Content** | The headless storefront may present approved educational, merchandising, and experience content while preserving Shopify-controlled commerce truth. |
| **Supplier Content** | Supplier assets and descriptions require review and normalization before publication. |
| **Legal and Policy Content** | Published terms, returns, warranties, privacy, and other policy commitments shall align with the applicable approved policy authority. |
| **AI-Assisted Content** | AI may assist drafting and transformation only within SSES-005 authority and shall not invent claims, specifications, citations, or owner decisions. |
 
# 15. Freshness, Review, and Retirement
- Content shall be reviewed when a linked product is discontinued, materially revised, or replaced.
- Time-sensitive claims, pricing references, incentives, lead times, regulations, research summaries, and seasonal statements shall include an appropriate review trigger.
- Broken links, superseded citations, inaccurate specifications, and contradictory guidance shall be corrected promptly.
- Content that no longer serves a distinct purpose shall be consolidated, redirected, archived, or retired according to the applicable information architecture and repository rules.
- Historical content retained for reference shall be labeled when its information is no longer current.
 
# 16. Quality and Acceptance Criteria
Content is ready for publication only when all applicable criteria are satisfied:
- The page has a defined audience, purpose, and next useful action.
- Product and technical facts have been verified against an approved source.
- Health, safety, and performance claims are proportionate to evidence and include necessary qualifications.
- The content follows approved voice, terminology, hierarchy, and readability rules.
- Material limitations, costs, installation needs, and suitability constraints are visible.
- The content does not duplicate another page without a distinct purpose.
- Links, citations, units, variants, and referenced policies are accurate.
- The content is usable on responsive layouts and does not depend on visual formatting alone to convey meaning.
- The human-readable and Claude Markdown sections of this specification remain synchronized.
- Owner approval is explicit for the approved specification.
 
# 17. Content Directives
## MUST
- Write for guest understanding and informed decision-making.
- Verify material specifications, claims, and source references before publication.
- Distinguish evidence, interpretation, tradition, and experience.
- Disclose material limitations and ownership requirements.
- Maintain consistent terminology across the storefront.
- Escalate unresolved claim, policy, product-data, or authority conflicts.
 
## SHOULD
- Create durable educational assets that can support multiple relevant journeys without duplicating content.
- Use examples, comparisons, diagrams, tables, and glossaries when they improve comprehension.
- Connect educational content to appropriate products without turning every section into a sales pitch.
- Write so a guest can understand the essential decision without relying on prior pages or hidden context.
 
## MUST NOT
- Invent product specifications, citations, study findings, testimonials, guarantees, or owner decisions.
- Publish supplier copy without verification and editorial review.
- Use fear, shame, false scarcity, or unsupported urgency to drive conversion.
- Present wellness content as individualized diagnosis or treatment.
- Use disclaimers to preserve wording that is otherwise misleading.
- Allow keyword targets to override accuracy, readability, or distinct page purpose.
 
# 18. Owner Directive
Soothing Saunas content exists to answer the practical question: **"How can we help you feel better?"**
 
Every page shall earn its place by making a guest more informed, more confident, or better supported. Content shall never trade trust for a faster conversion.
 
**Does this genuinely help someone live a healthier, happier life?**
